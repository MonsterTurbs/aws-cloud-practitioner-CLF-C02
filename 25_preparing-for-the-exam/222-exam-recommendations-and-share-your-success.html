Section 25 — Preparing for the Exam

Exam Recommendations & Share Your Success

A beginner-friendly reviewer to help you take the AWS Certified Cloud Practitioner (CLF‑C02) exam calmly and efficiently.

⏱️ Time management 📝 Mark for review strategy 🎯 Passing mindset ✅ Smart guessing

Print tip: Use your browser’s print dialog and keep margins enabled. This page is A4-ready.

1) When are you “ready” for the real exam?

Simple rule: If you finished the course videos + slides + the extra official AWS resources, you’re in a strong position to take the exam. ✅

Do this first (recommended): Take the practice exam included in the course. It’s free, it checks your knowledge, and it helps you get comfortable with the question style. 🙂

Use the practice exam results to identify weak topics, then review those lecture sections (and their slides) again.

2) Exam time & pacing (don’t rush)

The exam gives you 90 minutes. For an entry-level exam, that’s usually plenty of time if you stay calm. ⏳

  • Do not rush. Read carefully and eliminate wrong answers.
  • Do not get stuck. If a question is taking too long, use “mark for review.”
  • Micro-breaks are OK. Reset your focus for a few seconds and continue.

Common mistake: Spending too much time on one hard question early, then feeling pressured later. Avoid that by using the review feature. 📌

3) The “Mark for Review” strategy

If you are unsure about a question, don’t burn time. Use this workflow:

Step 1 — Choose a temporary answer 🤔

Pick the best option you can right now (even if you’re not fully sure).

Step 2 — Mark it for review 🏷️

Flag it so you can return after finishing easier questions.

Step 3 — Come back at the end 🔁

After the full pass, revisit marked questions with a fresher mind.

Why this works: Later questions sometimes remind you of key definitions (e.g., service purpose), which can make earlier questions easier when you return to them.

4) You don’t need 100% (and you should still answer everything)

To pass, you typically need about ~70% correct answers overall. 🎯

No penalty for guessing: If you truly don’t know the answer, guess. A guess has a chance of being correct; a blank has no chance. ✅

Beginner example

Let’s say you narrowed a question down to two choices. Even if you’re not sure, a 50/50 guess is much better than leaving it unanswered.

5) Quick exam-day checklist

  • ✅ Sleep and eat normally (avoid “cramming all night”).
  • ✅ Read the question carefully: single answer vs multiple answers.
  • ✅ Look for keywords: “most cost-effective,” “high availability,” “least operational overhead,” “best security posture,” etc.
  • ✅ Use elimination: remove obviously wrong services/options first.
  • ✅ Mark and move on when unsure.
  • ✅ Review flagged questions before submitting.

Mindset tip: This is a foundational exam. The goal is to recognize what services are for (and why the cloud is beneficial), not to design complex production architectures.

6) Share your success

After you pass, it’s a good idea to document your milestone (it can motivate you to continue with the next certification). 🎉

  • 🧾 Keep a copy of your result email / certification badge information.
  • 📌 Write down what topics felt hardest (so you can strengthen them later).
  • 📣 If you want, share your success on social media (e.g., Twitter/X) and tag your course instructor.

Optional: Share your win and your study strategy (what worked for you). This helps other learners too. 🙂

Mini reviewer (fast recall)

Before the exam, make sure you can explain these in 1–2 sentences each:

  • ☁️ Why cloud? (pay-as-you-go, agility, elasticity, global reach)
  • 🔐 Shared Responsibility Model
  • 👤 IAM basics (users, groups, roles, policies)
  • 🧰 Core services: EC2, S3, RDS, Lambda, VPC
  • 💸 Cost basics: Cost Explorer, Budgets, consolidated billing (Org)
  • 🛡️ Security monitoring: CloudTrail, GuardDuty
