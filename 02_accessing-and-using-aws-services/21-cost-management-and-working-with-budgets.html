21 — Cost Management & Working with Budgets 💳📊

Reviewer / Study Guide (single-column, A4 print-ready)

🎯 Lecture Goal

Learn how to monitor AWS costs and avoid surprise charges using the Billing Dashboard, Bills view, Cost Explorer, and Budgets.

Big idea ✅ Pricing knowledge + Free Tier is helpful, but you must still monitor usage and costs regularly to avoid unexpected bills.

🧭 Where to Monitor Costs in the Console

In the AWS Management Console, you can open the billing area like this:

  1. 1) Click your account name (top-right corner)
  2. 2) Click Billing Dashboard

What you’ll see 💡 Your current month bill information, even before the month ends.

📌 Billing Dashboard (Your Monthly Snapshot)

The lecture explains that the Billing Dashboard helps you track costs for the current month while the month is still ongoing.

  • 📈 View forecast for this month
  • 💸 See costs incurred so far
  • 🏷️ Identify which service is costing the most

Beginner reassurance ✅ If you follow the course steps and stay within Free Tier limits, you should avoid large bills.

🧾 Bills (Detailed Breakdown)

To go deeper, you can click Bills in the billing area.

  • 📥 Download bills (when available)
  • 🗓️ Choose a billing period (current month or past months)
  • 🧩 Check Charges by service to see which services cost money
  • 🌍 Drill down into cost details such as which regions incurred expenses

Big value ✅ “Charges by service” answers: “Which AWS service is making my bill grow?”

🛠️ Other Cost Management Tools You’ll See

The left-side billing menu includes more tools and settings, such as:

  • 💳 Payment details (update your credit card, etc.)
  • 📊 Cost management tools (Cost Explorer, Budgets)

📊 Cost Explorer (Know This for the Exam)

Cost Explorer helps you analyze and filter costs, especially when your AWS usage becomes more complex.

✅ When it helps most

  • When you use multiple services
  • When you need to understand where money is being spent

🔎 What you can filter by

  • 🌍 Region
  • 🧩 Service type
  • 🏷️ Tags (labels you can assign to resources/services)
  • ➕ And more

Exam focus ✅ You don’t need to use Cost Explorer in detail, but you should recognize what it is and why it exists.

🎯 Budgets (Alerts for Spending)

Budgets let you define a target spending limit (e.g., $10/month) and get warned when you’re close to it.

How it works (from the lecture) 🧪

  1. 1) Create a cost budget and give it a name
  2. 2) Set an amount you want to spend in a month (example: $10)
  3. 3) Configure alerts (example: email when you reach 80%)
  4. 4) If alerted, investigate via Cost Explorer or Bills
  5. 5) Stop the service that is driving costs

Important ⚠️ Budgets do not automatically block spending. You can still exceed the budget—it’s an alert system, not a hard limit.

Why budgets matter ✅ Budgets help prevent surprise charges by warning you early, so you can take action.

🧠 Recommended Routine (Simple Habit)

Based on the lecture’s guidance, a good habit is to check your costs regularly:

  • 📅 Open Billing Dashboard weekly (or even daily when experimenting)
  • 🔎 If costs rise, use Bills → Charges by service
  • 📊 Use Cost Explorer when the account becomes more complex
  • 🎯 Use Budgets to receive email alerts before you overspend

🗂️ Key Terms (Exam Focus)

Billing Dashboard Bills Forecast Charges by service Cost Explorer Budgets Tags Cost Alerts

✅ Quick Self-Check

Q1: How do you open the Billing Dashboard?

A: Click your account name (top-right) → Billing Dashboard.

Q2: What does the Billing Dashboard show during the month?

A: Forecast and costs incurred so far, plus which services are driving costs.

Q3: Where can you see “Charges by service”?

A: In the Bills section of the billing area.

Q4: What is Cost Explorer used for?

A: Deep cost analysis and filtering (by region, service, tags, etc.).

Q5: Do AWS Budgets prevent spending automatically?

A: No. Budgets alert you (e.g., email at 80%) but do not block charges.
