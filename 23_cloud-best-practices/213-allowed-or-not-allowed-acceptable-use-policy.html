✅ What is the AWS Acceptable Use Policy?

The AWS Acceptable Use Policy (AUP) is a simple document that explains what you are NOT allowed to do using AWS. Think of it as: “AWS is powerful — but you must use it responsibly and legally.”

🧠 Exam hint

If an exam question asks: “Where do you check what is allowed or not allowed on AWS?” → the expected answer is the Acceptable Use Policy.

🚫 What kinds of activities are NOT allowed?

The lecture highlights the obvious (but important) idea: you can’t use AWS for illegal or fraudulent activities, or for violating other people’s rights.

Common “not allowed” categories (high-level)

  • ⚖️ Illegal activity: anything that breaks laws/regulations applicable to you.
  • 🕵️ Fraud / deception: scams, impersonation, or dishonest activity using AWS resources.
  • 🧾 Rights violations: content or actions that infringe others’ rights (e.g., certain intellectual property misuse).
  • 🧨 Harmful use: actions that could cause harm to others, services, or systems.

Key takeaway: The AUP is the “red line” document. If you cross it, AWS can take action.

🧩 Simple examples (beginner-friendly)

✅ Allowed (normal usage)

  • Hosting a website or API for your app.
  • Storing backups in S3 for your project.
  • Running a database (RDS) for a legitimate business system.
  • Analyzing logs and metrics for your application.

🚫 Not allowed (AUP violations)

  • Using AWS to run scams or fraudulent services.
  • Using AWS resources to support illegal activity.
  • Purposely violating others’ rights through your hosted content or actions.

Note: The real AUP includes more detail and specific categories. For the exam, you mainly need the idea and where to find it.

⚠️ What can happen if you violate the AUP?

The lecture emphasizes that violations can lead to serious consequences.

  • 🔒 Account closure (your AWS account can be shut down).
  • ⛔ Being banned (losing access to AWS services).
  • 📌 Additional consequences depending on what happened (potentially beyond AWS actions).

Practical mindset: Treat AUP as a non-negotiable baseline for responsible cloud usage.

🧠 Quick reviewer: likely exam question

Q: Where can you check what you are allowed to do (or not allowed to do) on AWS?

A: The AWS Acceptable Use Policy.

Q: Why does AWS mention this in a best-practices section?

A: Because violating the AUP can shut down your account and block your ability to operate workloads in AWS.

🧾 Mini checklist: best-practice behavior

  • 📚 Know that the AUP exists and where to find it.
  • 🧰 Use AWS tools (Well-Architected Tool, Trusted Advisor, security services) to continuously improve your environment.
  • 🔍 Review your architecture periodically and remove risky or non-compliant behaviors early.

Wrap-up: If you follow the best practices and remain within the AUP, you can safely keep improving and scaling workloads in AWS. ✅
