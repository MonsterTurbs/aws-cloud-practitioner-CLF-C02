08 — Cloud Advantage: Global Reach 🌍

Reviewer / Study Guide (single-column, A4 print-ready)

🎯 Lecture Goal

Understand why AWS’s global network of data centers is a major cloud advantage and how it helps with latency, availability, and legal requirements.

Big idea ✅ AWS has data centers worldwide, so you can place workloads closer to users, meet data residency rules, and design for high availability across regions.

🌍 What “Global Reach” Means

Global reach means you can use AWS’s worldwide infrastructure to make your services:

  • 🌐 Available to more customers around the world
  • ⚡ Faster for customers by choosing closer locations (lower latency)
  • ✅ More resilient by running in multiple regions

✅ High Availability (Built on Top of the Cloud)

AWS provides tools and services that let you build systems that stay available even when something fails.

  • 🏗️ AWS operates multiple data centers across many locations.
  • 🚨 If one data center (or even a group) has issues, workloads can run in other regions.
  • 🧩 This helps your product avoid “sudden disappearance” due to infrastructure problems.

Beginner note 💡 High availability is not “magic.” You design for it by spreading workloads and using AWS services that support redundancy.

⚡ Lower Latency (Better User Experience)

Because AWS has multiple locations worldwide, you can choose the best location(s) for your workload.

What is latency? ⏱️

Latency is the delay between a user request and the response. Farther distance usually means higher latency.

Simple example 💡

If your users are in Asia and your servers are only in Europe, the site may feel slower. Hosting closer to users typically reduces the delay.

⚖️ Legal Requirements (Data Residency)

Sometimes you must store/process data in specific locations due to laws or compliance rules.

Data residency ✅ AWS’s global footprint helps you choose regions that align with local requirements (for example, “data must remain in country/region”).

✅ Quick Self-Check

Q1: What is the main advantage of AWS’s global network?

A: You can run workloads closer to customers, improve availability with multiple regions, and meet location/legal requirements.

Q2: Why does global reach help with high availability?

A: If one data center/region has problems, you can run workloads in other regions so the service stays up.

Q3: What does lower latency usually mean for users?

A: Faster response times and a smoother experience.

Q4: What is data residency?

A: A requirement that data must be stored/processed in a specific country or region for legal/compliance reasons.
