04 — A World Without Cloud Computing 🏢

Reviewer / Study Guide (single-column, A4 print-ready)

🎯 Lecture Goal

Understand the advantages and disadvantages of running your own data center (traditional / on-premise) so you can clearly see why companies move to cloud providers like AWS.

Big idea ✅ Cloud benefits are easier to appreciate when you compare them to the responsibilities of owning and operating your own infrastructure.

🌍 Why Every Modern Company Uses IT Resources

  • Almost every company today uses IT systems for websites, apps, analytics, and data processing.
  • Even companies that sell physical products (example: car makers) use the cloud for machine learning, analytics, and data storage.

Example 💡 A car company may need cars to communicate with cloud services to upload driving data and receive software updates.

🏢 Option A: Run Your Own Data Center (No Cloud)

In a world without cloud computing, a company may:

  • Buy servers, storage, networking equipment 🖥️
  • Host them inside its own building(s) (data center) 🏗️
  • Run workloads directly on that hardware

✅ Advantages of Owning Your Own Infrastructure

  • 🛠️ Full control over hardware and physical setup.
  • 📍 You know exactly where the computers and data are located (can matter for legal/compliance reasons).
  • 🧾 Your data and hardware are not tied to a third-party provider’s business situation.

Beginner note 💡 These benefits sound attractive, but they come with major operational responsibilities.

⚠️ Disadvantages (Why This is Hard and Expensive)

1) Maintenance is your job 🔧

  • You maintain servers, networking, power, cooling, and replacements.
  • Costs and complexity grow as you scale beyond “a few machines.”

2) Capacity planning (long-term) 📊

  • You must predict future demand (traffic, requests, data growth).
  • Upgrading is slow: buy hardware, ship it, install it, configure it.

3) Security of the physical infrastructure 🛡️

  • You must protect the facility from disasters (storms, floods) 🌧️
  • You must protect against intrusion or insider threats 🚪

4) The “too much vs too little” capacity problem ⚖️

  • If you have too little capacity, you can’t handle spikes (slow site, downtime).
  • If you have too much capacity, you pay for idle servers most of the time.

Simple example 💡 You buy 10 servers “just in case,” but you only need 3 most days—7 servers sit idle while still costing money.

5) Limited locations = latency + disaster risk 🌐

  • Most companies have only one/few data center locations.
  • Customers far away may experience higher latency (slower response).
  • A major catastrophe in that location can take everything down.

➡️ Why Companies Move to the Cloud

  • Cloud providers can reduce maintenance burden (they manage infrastructure).
  • Cloud resources can be scaled faster (less waiting for hardware purchases).
  • Cloud regions around the world help reduce latency and improve resilience.

Key takeaway ✅ Cloud computing helps address on-prem problems: maintenance, scaling delays, capacity planning stress, and location limitations.

🗂️ Key Terms to Remember

On-Premise Data Center Capacity Planning Workload Spike Excess Capacity Latency Infrastructure Security Cloud Provider

✅ Quick Self-Check

Q1: What is one advantage of owning your own data center?

A: Full control over the physical infrastructure and knowing exactly where data/hardware are located.

Q2: Why is capacity planning difficult on-prem?

A: Demand changes, but buying and installing hardware takes time—so you often end up with too much or too little capacity.

Q3: What does “latency” mean in this context?

A: The delay users experience when they are far from your data center (slower responses).

Q4: Give one reason cloud providers help with disaster risk.

A: They operate across multiple locations/regions, which can reduce reliance on one single site.
