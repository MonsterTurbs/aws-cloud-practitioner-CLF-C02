Business Applications on AWS — Reviewer / Study Guide

Focus: WorkMail, Honeycode, Chime • Single-column • A4 print-ready

🏢 Business tools 🧰 Not for building AWS products 📝 Usually not tested (CLF-C02)

1) Big Picture: What are “Business Applications” in AWS?

AWS has some services that behave like company productivity tools (email, meetings/chat, no-code apps). These are different from core cloud services (EC2, S3, RDS, VPC) that you typically use to build products.

Key takeaway 🧠

These services are mainly for internal company usage (like using email or a meeting tool), not primarily for building your own customer-facing cloud architecture.

For the CLF-C02 exam, these are typically low-priority. Still, it helps to know what they are at a high level.

2) The Services You Should Recognize

📧 Amazon WorkMail Email & Calendar

What it is: AWS’s managed business email and calendaring service.

Think: an AWS alternative to Google Workspace (Gmail) or Microsoft Exchange.

  • Used for internal communications: email inboxes, calendars, contacts.
  • Not typically used as a “building block” to create cloud architectures like EC2/S3.
Beginner example ✉️

A small company wants company-name.com email addresses (e.g., it@company-name.com) without running their own mail servers. They can use WorkMail to get managed mailboxes.

🧩 Amazon Honeycode No-code Apps

What it is: A no-code environment that lets you build simple apps without writing code.

Think: “build an internal tool quickly” (forms, tables, workflows) without needing a full dev team.

  • Good for internal apps like trackers, approval workflows, simple dashboards.
  • Designed to help non-developers (or busy teams) ship tools faster.
Beginner example 🗂️

HR needs a simple app to track onboarding tasks (ID badge, laptop, orientation schedule). With Honeycode, they can create a checklist app without writing JavaScript or building a database.

🎥 Amazon Chime Meetings & Chat

What it is: A communication service for online meetings, video calls, and chat.

Think: an alternative to Microsoft Teams, Google Meet, or Slack (depending on the feature set).

  • Used by teams to collaborate (calls, screen sharing, messaging).
  • Again: mainly an internal productivity tool, not a typical cloud “architecture building block”.
Beginner example 🧑‍💼

A remote team needs daily standups and screen sharing for troubleshooting. They can run meetings on Chime instead of using a third-party video platform.

3) Quick “When to Use What” Map

  • Need company email/calendar? → 📧 WorkMail
  • Need a simple internal app without coding? → 🧩 Honeycode
  • Need meetings/chat collaboration? → 🎥 Chime

4) Quick Check ✅ (Exam-Style Recall)

  1. True or False: WorkMail is mainly used to build customer-facing cloud architectures.
    Answer: False — it’s a business email/calendar tool.
  2. Which service is “no-code” for building simple apps?
    Answer: Honeycode 🧩
  3. You need a tool like Teams/Meet for video meetings: which AWS service fits best?
    Answer: Chime 🎥

5) Mini Glossary

  • Business application: a tool used by employees to work (email, meetings, internal apps).
  • No-code: building apps using visual tools instead of programming languages.
  • Internal tool: software used inside a company (not typically sold to customers).
